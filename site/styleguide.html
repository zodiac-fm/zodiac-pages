Part Two

THE RULES

Everything that governs a page once the frames are chosen.

Chapter 1

THE FILTER — THE THIRTEEN

Every frame passes these before it ships. The numbers are the floor; rule 13 is the gate.

  1. Kill the colour and look — if nothing holds your eye in grey, colour will not rescue it.
  2. The coloured thing must stand out even in grey.
  3. When a colour looks weak, fix the grey behind it — never the saturation.
  4. Never desaturate, always convert — keep the colour master.
  5. Colour sits on top of the photograph's own light — a flat inside is paint.
  6. Point at what is making the colour — no source, no colour.
  7. Quiet everything, let one thing sing.
  8. Stand close — the edge gives a fake away.
  9. Same texture on both sides of the line.
  10. Real colour lands on what sits nearby.
  11. Write the recipe down or it will not happen twice.
  12. Make a calmer version and let them fight.
  13. Name the light on a person — colour may kiss skin, never paint it.
Chapter 2

LIGHT

  1. Start bright — frames and pages open in light.
  2. Light is made into the photo at creation, never painted after.
  3. Every light has a pointable source.
  4. Faces take warm key light only; a real reflection may kiss skin; frequency colour may never paint it.
  5. Amber is our sunlight, not a pop.
Chapter 3

TEXTURE

  1. Texture is photographed, never tiled.
  2. Busy carries no words — copy sits on a calm zone, always.
  3. One grain, frame and page; type is the one exemption.
  4. Texture change announces the section — no divider lines.
  5. Sharpness is hierarchy — crisp only where the eye should land.
Chapter 4

FURNITURE charts, bullets, captions

Master law
Colour lives in the mark, not the font — coloured words are a rare deliberate move, on Michael's taste, never a habit.
Charts
  • Ink on paper.
  • A bar wears band colour only when it IS that frequency.
  • Numbers in Switzer, never the serif.
  • One chart style per screen.
  • Ground stays paper, grid whisper-grey.
Bullets
  • A band-coloured mark at full intensity, ink words.
Captions
  • Quiet ink under the photo; a small chip only when the photo belongs to a frequency.

Chapter 6

BULLETS — DICTATED

One law, two built forms. The mark carries the colour and the words stay ink — so a bullet is a construction question, not a colour question.

Form one — the step mark measured on d4-l
  • Shape — a pill: 18px wide, 4px tall, 2px corner radius, display:inline-block.
  • Colour — the band token at full intensity, set inline: background:var(--core). No tint, no opacity, no gradient.
  • Gap to text — 8px, carried by the mark's own margin-right, not by a flex gap.
  • Seating — vertical-align:2px: the pill rides two pixels above the baseline so it centres on the cap height of an 11px uppercase kicker.
  • Text — Switzer 600, 11px, .18em, uppercase. Same at 1440 and at 375; d4-l does not step it down.
  • Spacing below — the kicker carries margin-bottom:12px to the head that follows (16px in the music chapter).
Step one · core Step two · vitality Step three · abundance
Form two — the band dot measured on d4-l
  • Shape — a circle: 7×7px, 50% radius, on the song card's band line.
  • Colour — the card's own band variable at full intensity; the line's words take the band's ink variable.
  • Gap to text — 6px, a flex gap. Text — Switzer 600, 12.5px.
Core Love Vitality Abundance
When the mark rotates, and when it holds one band
  • Measured Rotates when the sibling items each are a different frequency. d4-l's three-step row runs core → vitality → abundance, one band per step, in canon order with love skipped because there are three steps and four bands. A short row skips from the end of the order; it never repeats a band inside one row.
  • Measured Holds one band inside a single band's own object. Every dot on one song card is that card's band, for the whole component.
  • Proposed · Iris lane A list that is not about frequency at all takes one neutral mark and never rotates. Rotating band colour through a list that carries no frequency meaning is decoration, and decoration is the thing this system is built to refuse. This is the Iris lane's call; d4-l has no such list to measure.
  • Proposed · Iris lane The neutral mark: a 16×2px bar at 34px indent, seated at 1.22em — the mark this review surface uses on its own lists. Unmeasured on any live page.
Chapter 7

CAPTIONS — DICTATED

Measured on d4-l
  • Position — always below the frame, margin-top:14px. No rule, no box, no tint behind it.
  • Size and colour — scene captions 14.5px in --ink56 (.66), the lead half in --ink at 600. Object captions 15px / 1.5 in --ink56, their first line a block title in --ink at 600, 16.5px.
  • The chip rule — the frequency waveform sits first, then the words: 96×20px, object-fit:contain, left-aligned, nudged 3px down so it seats on the text baseline, with a 12px gap to the words and the whole line aligned align-items:baseline. One chip, one frequency, only when the photo belongs to a band.
  • Max-width — d4-l caps the caption by its column, not by a property: the scenes grid is 1040px across two columns with a 40px gutter, so a caption measures about 500px at 1440. The one explicit cap on the page is 520px on the lead music caption.
  • At 375 — captions do not step down. 14.5px stays 14.5px, the chip stays 96×20. d4-l steps headings and stats at 640px and leaves captions alone.
Measured on this review surface — and the conflict
  • The library's own figcaption: border-top 1px --hair, margin-top 14px, padding-top 12px, Switzer 400 14px / 1.5 in --cream58; the recipe line 14.5px / 1.6 in --cream82; the P-number 600 30px (26px at 760 and below); the title 600 19px (17px).
  • Conflict, and d4-l wins. The review surface puts a hairline above every caption. d4-l puts nothing above a caption. On a live page there is no rule above a caption — the hairline is a review device that exists to seat the big P-number under the label law, and it stays on the review surfaces only.
Proposed — Iris lane, unmeasured
  • Proposed Cap the caption at 34em, and never wider than the frame above it. d4-l gets this from its grid; a caption under a full-bleed frame has no grid to protect it.
  • Proposed One caption, one job. Either a lead in ink plus a lighter descriptive half on one line (d4-l's scene form), or a block title plus a paragraph (d4-l's object form). Not both grammars in one chapter — the one-chart-grammar rule, applied to words.
Chapter 8

HOW TO BUILD A PAGE

In order. Each step closes before the next opens.

  1. List the sections, and write one line saying what each section is for.
  2. The ground is the paper. A section's mood — sun, neutral, night — comes from its photographs and their light, and the ground-pop law binds whatever sits behind a pop.
  3. Pick the treatments: one SOLO per section, and CHORD only at the bookends, opener and finale.
  4. Pull the frames from the library, or write the making-prompt for what is missing — the light is made at creation, never painted after.
  5. Set the type from the tables above; nothing on the page gets a size that is not in them.
  6. Place the furniture by its own rules — charts, bullets, captions.
  7. Run the thirteen on every frame, and the squint test on every ground-and-pop pair.
  8. QA both viewports down to the 375 floor, then make the calmer version and let the two fight before anything is shown.
Chapter 9

TOKENS

TOKENS = the only colours that exist. Everything else is a mistake with a hex code.

design/tokens.json is the single source for the band tokens. Nothing on a page gets a colour that is not read out of that file. The four bands are the whole wheel; the paper and the ink are the world they arrive in.

The four wheel tokens
Core violet · #6521C9 Love rose · #B90044 Vitality amber · #FF9112 Abundance emerald · #12A56E
  • Full intensity or nothing. A band token is used at the value above. There is no tint of a band, no opacity of a band, no lightened band. If it needs to be quieter, it becomes ink.
  • A band appears when the content is that band. Violet is the site's working accent because most content is Core; the other three arrive when the words beside them are theirs.
  • Roughly nine parts paper and ink to one part colour. The valley is what makes the pop detonate — the same law the frames run on, applied to the page.
The site ground
Paper · #F0F0EF Ink · #141412
  • --paper #F0F0EF — the resting ground, true neutral. Pages are built on it. Sections do not swap it; a section's mood comes from its photographs and their light.
  • --ink #141412 — type, marks, chart bars. The reading opacities and the measured type values are in Chapter 5; use the token, never the number in its name.
  • --hair rgba(20,20,18,.12) — one hairline, the only divider the system owns.
The dark stage tokens are the APP world. #0B0712 and the panel colours beside it belong to the product interface, and to review surfaces like this one; the SITE ground is the paper. A site page never borrows the app's ground.
Law
Colour comes out of the token file, never out of a pixel.

Chapter 10

TYPE VOICES — AND NUMBERS

TWO VOICES = the serif speaks, Switzer works.

Two typefaces, and the division between them is a job description, not a taste. The measured sizes for every element are dictated in Chapter 5; this chapter is the rule that decides which face an element gets in the first place.

  1. Cormorant Garamond carries the big human statements. The headline, the section head, the pull quote, the testimonial. The places a person is being spoken to.
  2. Switzer carries everything functional. Body, ledes, kickers, labels, captions, chart furniture, links, buttons, fine print. The places the page is doing a job.
  3. Numbers are always Switzer. The serif never touches a digit, at any size, anywhere — inside a serif quote, a figure switches face mid-sentence rather than break the law.
  4. Ink on paper. Type does not get a colour to make it interesting; colour lives in the mark beside it.
  5. The wordmark is the real asset, never re-typeset.
What a number looks like
  • Data stays in digits — 300 people, 52 weeks, 7 measured outcomes.
  • Small counts that are voice may be spelled — four frequencies, two voices. Never both treatments in one sentence.
  • Dates are written out — August 13, 2026. No numeric shorthand, no ordinal suffixes; compact UI may shorten the month.
  • A unit whispers, the figure is the hero — the per-cent sign and the Hz sit small and quiet beside their number.
  • A stat label sits under its number, never above it, four words at most.
Fonts are trial licenses. Cormorant Garamond and Switzer are on trial and the work they appear in is a mockup. Nothing typeset in them ships to a paying surface until the licence call is made.
Law
Numbers always Switzer, never the serif.
Law
The serif speaks; Switzer works.
Chapter 11

PARAGRAPHS, LISTS, LENGTHS

COPY GRAMMAR = five ways a paragraph exists, four ways a list exists, and a ceiling on every one of them.

The design assumes these lengths. Copy that runs past them does not stretch the page — it goes back to the copy lane. A builder never invents a sixth paragraph form.

The five paragraph forms
  • The lede. One idea, opening a section. At most one per section, and optional. “Music tuned to the moment you were born, playing under your whole day.”
  • The body paragraph. One to three sentences, ink on paper, air between them. The air is part of the design, not a gap to fill.
  • The statement. Serif italic, one sentence, at most one per section. It is the only place a paragraph is allowed to be dramatic. “You have been listening to everyone else's frequency.”
  • The long-form paragraph. Reading pages only — FAQ answers, the story, policies. It may carry a full thought past the marketing ceiling, because the reader who reached a reading page has already decided to read. A subhead lands every three to four paragraphs so the column never becomes a wall.
  • The note. Fine print, caveats, sources. Quiet ink, never load-bearing — except a caveat that must travel with a claim, which sits one step darker so it cannot be skimmed past.
The four list forms
  • The step mark — benefits and rules. The band-coloured pill, ink words, built to the measured values in Chapter 6. Three to six items, twelve words each.
  • The counter — sequences. Numbered steps the reader will actually follow, the figures in Switzer.
  • The definition list — features and facts. Term left, description right; two or three words in the term, twenty-five in the description; the pair stacks on the phone.
  • The FAQ — questions. Its own grammar, in Chapter 12.
Two reading modes, two widths
  • Short copy is a marketing section: a head, two paragraphs, a list, one button, composed on the page grid. The whole section reads in under thirty seconds.
  • Long copy is a reading page: one centred column at 700px, left-aligned, the type relaxed and subheads pacing the scroll. Nothing on a reading page runs wider than that column.
  • Everywhere: text measures at most 34em. No line of copy ever spans the full grid. Centred text is for short heads and compact closers, never for long copy.
  • At the floor: the phone column is judged at 375×812, its own decision every time. Mobile is never a scaled desktop.

Chapter 12

FAQ GRAMMAR

FAQ = questions in their voice, answers in ours.

  1. The question is written the way a customer asks it — first person, plain, twelve words at most. Not the way we would phrase the topic.
  2. The answer is ours — the banked Short from the canonical answer bank, never improvised on the page. The Long version lives on the support page.
  3. The hardest question goes first, open. Hiding the objection is how a page loses the reader who was closest to buying.
  4. Five to eight questions on a page. Past eight it is a support page pretending to be a section.
  5. Closed by default, one open at a time; the plus mark rotates to a close. The mark is the only moving part.
  6. An answer that needs a caveat carries the caveat inside the answer, not in a note below it.
Answers come from the bank. If a question has no banked answer, the question does not ship. Writing one on the page is how a promise nobody approved enters the world.
Chapter 13

APP SCREENS

APP SCREENS = real screens, shown as objects of light.

This is CHIP at phone scale — the treatment where the product's own marks carry the colour, in the one place they are already carrying it. On a page made of paper and silver, a live screen is the loudest colour there is, so it stands alone and needs nothing behind it.

  1. Real captures from staging, never mockups. The site can then never promise an interface that does not exist.
  2. The screen is the only full-colour object in its frame. Everything around it stays silver.
  3. Captured at the 375×812 floor, retina. One hairline edge, no drop shadow, no glow.
  4. Never placed over photography, never tilted, never perspective-warped.
  5. Waves are drawn by code, never generated. Every generated attempt rendered neon; the drawn filament is the only wave that ships, and it is a hairline, not a plume.
The frames for this already exist. P-162 is the hero phone shot clean; P-109 is the object case. The CHIP chapter above shows the same mark doing its job at page scale.
Chapter 14

GRADIENTS app-UI grammar, scoped

GRADIENTS = button grammar that lives inside a real screen.

The gradient system is real, it is in design/tokens.json, and the app inherits it unchanged. It is included here for one reason: so nobody carries it out of the screen and onto the page. The site page itself never carries a gradient ground and never carries gradient text. A gradient appears on a site page only inside a screenshot of a screen that legitimately has one.

  • Linear only, token pairs only, 160deg by default.
  • Functional — primary to deep, one hue. Buttons, controls, active states; nearly all gradient use.
  • Frequency — primary to its own analogous end. A band's identity: drawn waves, tiles.
  • Brand — one primary to a different primary. Identity moments only, never inside app UI, and rare.
The never-list
  • No radial glow behind content. The dark-void-plus-violet-glow look is the generic one; it was killed and stays killed.
  • No gradient wash over photography. A photograph's colour is carried by something in the frame, never applied on top of it.
  • No gradient text. Type is ink, flat.
  • No gradient page grounds. The ground is the paper. Air and one hairline make the depth.
If you can see a gradient on a site page and it is not inside a screen, it is a bug.

THE GROUND NEVER SCREWS WITH THE POP

Rule 3 governs whatever sits behind a pop: the ground steps back to clean near-silver inside the field-tint cap. Warmth beyond about a point exists only inside a pointable lit passage, never in the resting ground. Every ground-and-pop pairing passes the kill-the-colour squint before it ships.
