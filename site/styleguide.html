The Zodiac Page System

Every Zodiac page is black-and-white photography on neutral paper. Colour arrives once per section, as one real thing or one real light, and only ever in one of four colours. That is the whole system. Everything below is how you place it.

Built from the live home page, home-t12, 2026-09-08. This guide is only ever as current as the page it names: every picture in it is a crop cut from that page as it stands today, and the next lock cuts them again.

Chapter 0

The four colours, and how to use this guide

There are four colours on a Zodiac page and no others. Three of them are things you can pick up. One of them is light.

The colours

Named for the four frequencies. One colour per section, and never the same colour in two sections that touch.

Core violetWho you are. The first colour of the set.
Love roseHow you relate. The proof sections' colour on the home page.
Vitality amberYour energy. This one is sunlight.
Abundance emeraldOpportunity. The colour of the open air.

Amber is our sunlight and it falls on things. The other three are things. Violet, rose and emerald arrive as an object you can point at — a ball, a dress, a record. Amber arrives as a rim on a shoulder, a glow in an earcup, a halation edge. Amber sitting on bright paper as an object of its own reads as a stain.

How to use this guide

  • Pick a page recipe. The home page is written out in full in the last chapter, and there is a rule there for writing a new one.
  • Place the sections. Chapter 3 is the catalogue of templates that already exist, with what each one is for and what content it takes.
  • Give each section its setting and its effect. The setting is the light it sits in (chapter 1). The effect is how colour gets onto it (chapter 4). One of each, per section.
  • Pull the pictures from the library by number. Never from a folder, never from an old round, never a retired number. The library is the one home for every picture: The Zodiac Picture Library.

House pattern for every paper: red equals green, blue one less, so no setting carries a hue.

Chapter 2

Lighting a page

The settings are not decoration. They are the order the reader moves through, and there are seven rules about how they can follow one another.

The rules

  • Neighbours never share a setting. Two sections on the same paper read as one long section, and the reader stops noticing they have moved.
  • Neighbouring light papers are a clear step apart. If you cannot see the join, it is not a join.
  • The night is one scene. If two chapters both belong in the dark, they become one section with parts inside it. Never two nights in a row.
  • Dawn only ever follows night. On its own it is just a white page. After the dark it is the lights coming up.
  • Into the night is a cut. Out of the night is a cut. Everywhere else the paper fades from the one above, and the fade is finished before the first line of text.
  • No warm pixel anywhere on the page. Not in the paper, the ink, a hairline or a frame. Only a lamp inside a photograph may be warm.
  • Faces are always silver. Colour never lands on a person's skin — that is what the effects in chapter 4 exist to avoid.
For the builder

All seven are machine-checked: python3 code/check_page.py preflight <page>, python3 code/check_page.py warm <url>, python3 code/check_page.py ground <url>.

The join is one ramp at the top of each section, fading in from --above, which is derived from the previous sibling. A cut is --ramp:0 — and the zero must carry a unit, or the whole padding declaration is thrown away.

Step floor: at least 4.5 L* between neighbouring light papers. The night stays inside L* 22–34.

Chapter 4

The effects

How colour gets onto a section. There are three families: colour inside the photograph, colour marked on the type, and light thrown across the section itself. One effect per section, one colour per section, and neighbours never share a colour.

Group A · In the photograph

The colour is a real object, photographed. Choose pictures for these in the library — every frame there is filed by which of these it carries.

Solo

One real coloured object in an otherwise silver photograph, with the photograph's own light and shadow still inside the colour.

See it live

Chord

All four colours as four real things in one photograph. Use it at the two ends of a page and nowhere in between.

See it live

Through

Light passes through a coloured thing and the thing becomes the lamp: a record, a parasol, cloth, glass. This is the room's native effect.

See it live

Spill

The coloured thing lands a whisper of itself on the grey beside it. Always with solo or through — without the spill the object looks pasted on.

See it live

Worn

Colour as a real garment. This is the one way colour is allowed to touch a person at all — and it still never touches the face.

See it live

Sun

Amber as light and never as an object on bright paper: the rim on a shoulder, the halation edge, the glow inside a thing.

See it live

Group B · On the type

Highlight

One colour on the one line that carries the argument. Translucent and tapered, drawn on the words so it ends where the words end. Never a solid block, never a band across the section. At most two lines, usually one.

The credential line under a name may take the same mark far weaker, so the quote still leads. Nothing else in the section carries colour — the mark works because the field around it is empty.

See it live

For the builder

Copy #voices .lit and #voices .lit-faint from site/home-t12.html and swap the colour; the recipe does not change.

Two background layers on the text itself: a vertical wash of the section's own paper over the stroke's top and bottom edges, then a tapered gradient tilted about 101deg under it. box-decoration-break: clone so every line gets its own stroke. Keep the wash geometry in pixels (about 11 px) or a large heading blurs.

On the night, wash into the lit charcoal the mark actually sits on, not the declared rung, or the stroke gets a dark lip along both edges. The stroke's centre sits 5–7 L* above the lifted sheet.

Group C · Light on the section

Night and dawn only. Four things have to be present, all of them, or it is a tint rather than light: a source you can point at, falloff, rims on the frames facing it, and shadows falling away from it.

Sign light

Night. One coloured light from off the top edge, as if a sign hangs above the wall. The visitor sees a room with a lamp in it, not a coloured page. The words sit in the even part, below where the light is still moving.

See it live

Lit meter

Night, alongside the sign light. A chart's bars become thin lines in the section's colour, brighter than their track. The numbers and the labels stay white — the colour is in the bars and nowhere else.

See it live

First light

Dawn. A pool of the colour at the top of the whitest paper, as if the sun is just coming up. The rest of the paper stays pure white and the frames throw long soft shadows downward. Light cannot brighten white, so this is a small footprint at full strength — never a wash across the section.

See it live

For the builder

At the top edge the charcoal rises to L* 46–52 carrying chroma 26–36; an ease-out fall through the height of the voices to L* 30–32 by the numbers and plain charcoal at the foot. Never more than 6 L* across any 100 px, so the ground check passes under every block. Frame rim two stops: 1 px at L* 70–78 in the tint, 1 px at L* 50–56 inside it, on the edge facing the source. Shadow 10–14 px offset at phone, 16–22 at desktop, blur 20–30, at least 10 L* below the sheet.

Space and furniture

The left rail

On desktop, every heading on the page starts exactly where the hero headline starts. One vertical line runs the whole page, and nothing crosses it. Labels sit in the margin to the left of it; the heading, the paragraph and the pictures all begin on it.

The portrait frame

A frame sits one step above the paper it is on. Every face on a page is a square framed portrait — the whole head, a hairline border, a deliberate ground brighter than the section's own paper, and a soft shadow. No cut-out heads, no fading edges: those crop people badly and it looks wrong.

Faces are always silver. The frame may take a rim of the section's colour on the edge facing a light; the face never does.

Hairlines

One hairline weight on the whole page, in the page's own ink and never in a colour. It separates one voice from the next and seats a name under a quote. It is never a box: nothing on a Zodiac page is enclosed in a border.

For the builder

Rail. At 1440 the headline and every section heading both start at 182 px, inside a 1140 px shell with 32 px gutters; at 375 both start at 22 px. The section head is a grid of a 150 px label column and a 760 px text column above 900 px wide.

Frame. .fport: background #F1F1F0, border 1px rgba(20,20,18,.16), radius 3px, shadow 0 20px 44px rgba(20,20,18,.14) in proof sections. Image object-fit:cover; object-position:center top.

Hairline. 1px at rgba(20,20,18,.17) on the grey paper; --lv-hair resolves per setting, from .12 on the lightest papers to .18 white on the night.

Chapter 6

Never on a page

Eight things. Each one has been tried and each one broke the page.

  1. Coloured light from nowhereA tint, a filter, a gradient, a cone with no source. If you cannot point at what is making the colour, it is not light — it is a stain, and the reader feels it even if they cannot name it.
  2. A section or a box filled or tinted with colourFilling the field cancels the pop it belongs to. The colour only reads because everything around it is black and white; fill the field and there is nothing left for it to land against.
  3. Chips beside labelsSmall coloured markers next to a kicker or a step label. They look like a system and they are decoration. The colour belongs in the photograph or on the line of type, not in furniture.
  4. Cream, or any warm pixel in the chromePaper, ink, hairlines and frames are all neutral. Only a lamp inside a photograph may be warm.
  5. Colour on a faceNever, in any colour, at any strength. Faces are always silver. The one thing that may touch a person is a colour they are wearing.
  6. A painted or warped phoneA phone on any page is the library's registered device carrying a real screen. A drawn phone, a stretched screenshot or a mocked-up device is never used anywhere.
  7. A call to action on the home pageThere is a sentence and a plain text link. No button, no banner, no urgency.
  8. A retired pictureA retired number may stay on the pages that already carried it when it was retired, and may appear nowhere new — not from an old folder, not from an archive, not from a superseded round. The library lists every retired number and its replacement.

The rule for a new page

  • Write the section list first, in plain sentences: what each section is for, in the order a reader meets them.
  • Alternate the settings so no two neighbours match. Sun, room, sun, room is the opening rhythm that works.
  • Put the night where the proof is heaviest, and only once.
  • Put dawn straight after the night, and nowhere else.
  • End on sun.
  • Then give each section one colour and one effect, matched to its setting: the light papers carry colour inside their photographs; the room carries a photograph or a marked line; the night carries the sign light, a marked line and, if there is a chart, the lit bars; dawn carries first light and a marked line.
  • Neighbours never share a colour. Amber lights; it never sits.
  • Pull every picture from the library by number, and check it is not retired.

Chapter 8

The index for machines

Everything above is also written into this page as structured data, so a person or a program handed this address can read the whole system without scraping the layout.

What it is

A block of structured data at the end of this page lists every chapter and every card on it, each with its own link and a one-line summary. Each card on this page carries a permanent link of its own, so you can point someone straight at a single rule. The companion index of every picture — number, name, file, effect, colour and subject — lives on The Zodiac Picture Library.

For the builder

Read it with JSON.parse(document.getElementById('zfm-index').textContent). Shape: {page, built_from, built_on, colours[], settings[], effects[], chapters[{id, name, summary, cards[{id, name, summary}]}]}. Every id is an element id in this document.
