02 · Gradients

Three recipes, all linear, all from tokens

The gradient system already exists in design/tokens.json; the site inherits it unchanged. Every gradient is linear, built from locked tokens, and lives on a graphic surface: a button, a drawn wave, a small identity element. The page ground never becomes a gradient, and nothing glows.

Functionalprimary → deep, same hue · #6521C9 → #38007A · buttons, controls, active states. ~90% of gradient use.
Frequencyprimary → gradEnd, analogous · #6521C9 → #A737C8 · a frequency's own identity: drawn waves, tiles. Each band has its own gradEnd.
Brandprimary → a different primary · hero moments, marketing identity only. Never inside app UI, and rare on the site.
NeverWhy

Vertical spacing is contextual, never uniform. A major chapter opens with the full 120; standard sections breathe at 96; closely related blocks sit at 72. Long sales pages stop feeling stretched, and the big dramatic transition stays reserved for the moments that earn it.

  • The 1140px content frame is centered. The 700px reading column is centered inside it.
  • Paragraphs, lists, proof, forms, and CTAs sit left-aligned inside the reading column.
  • Centered text is reserved for short headings and compact closing statements, never long copy.
  • On mobile, every card and grid stacks single-column.
  • The testimonial photo drops to 150px.
  • One primary button per screen holds at both sizes.
  • Mobile is never a scaled desktop; every value above is its own decision.

Support · Getting Set Up
Do I need headphones?

Headphones are preferred but not necessary. What matters is that you use a speaker that lets the frequency come through cleanly, which means clear midrange, balanced sound, and low distortion at normal volume.

This means that any speaker that costs about $75 or more (JBL Flip or Charge, Bose SoundLink, Sonos Roam or Move, Marshall, a mid-tier Anker Soundcore), almost any car stereo, or any decent pair of headphones will be fine. Apple EarPods are excellent.

What to avoid

Phone speakers, laptop speakers, and very small cheap Bluetooth speakers. They strip the low-end warmth, over-process the audio, flatten the sound, and lose detail.

Then just listen while you walk, work, cook, or drive. What matters is how much you listen over time.

11 · FAQ

Questions in their voice, answers in ours

Questions are written the way customers ask them. On-site answers are the banked Short versions from the canonical answer bank; the full Long versions live on the support page. Both example answers below are BANKED and confirmed, and the third is placeholder to show a closed state.

I don't know my exact birth time. Can I still use it?

This is our most common question, so you are not alone. The answer is that you can absolutely use the product even if you do not know your birth time. Your birth time does in fact fine-tune a part of how we calculate your frequencies.

However, depending on your configuration, a couple of minutes or even a couple of hours will not dramatically affect your calculation. The perfect frequency is better, but if it is in range, it will still have an amplifying effect.

More importantly, if you are not getting an experience, we will work with you through trial and error until you get an undeniable experience. This includes expanding your refund window, just make sure to communicate.

Do I need headphones?

Headphones, like Apple EarPods, are preferred. But you can use speakers, as long as you pick a model that lets the frequency come through cleanly, which means clear midrange, balanced sound, and low distortion at normal volume. Any Bluetooth speaker that costs $75 or more is totally acceptable. Your car stereo will work just fine, too!

Just not phone or laptop speakers. They blur everything together, so the tone gets lost.

12 · The Photographic System

Five categories, one treatment. Come with a band, leave with a frame.

The scene is black and white. The color is the event.

The system is organized for retrieval. Every photograph belongs to one of five categories — the four frequencies, then multicolor — and every one is the same treatment. World of Color lets the world carry the hue: a parasol, a dress, a lit sign, or cast light landing on hands, hair, fabric, the environment. Cast light is a color source inside the treatment, not a second treatment. One wheel, five categories, the method chosen frame by frame.

  • Photos sit directly on the stage: 4px radius, no borders, no panels, no overlays. Full-width for hero moments, two-up for supporting frames.
  • The color is lit, never paint. One band per frame is the default; light needs no visible source, and an invented source is legal.
  • Cast light may land on hands, hair, fabric, and environment. A face takes a slight shine at most. A face is never the carrier.
  • More than one band: the Full Wheel appears as four objects, four people, or four lit events, never a wash; in group scenes each person may carry their own band.
  • Every frame audits onto the locked wheel spans before it ships.

13 · The Frequencies, In Context

The wave is drawn by code, never generated

Frequency is frozen. The old production references, wave E and water-3, fell in the 2026-08-12 eval pass; a dedicated lane owns cracking the wave. Until it lands, no drawn frequency ships anywhere on the site: no smoke, no wisps, no composited waves over photography. The one surviving direction is black and white and drier than smoke, a charcoal print, not a plume.

The filament divider

At interface scale the wave becomes a single code-drawn filament: an SVG whisper used as a section divider or a quiet signature near the footer. Hairline weight, violet, never animated to attract, never repeated down the page.

Filament · inline SVG · stroke 1.5px · violet-glint fading to nothing at both ends · one per page region · smoke-body waves stay in rendered frames, never in UI

Bases come from photography; the wave layer is composited in code with numeric gates (identity untouched, hue in band, clearance held). Gen-drawn waves are banned by evidence: every attempt rendered neon.

17 · The Laws

Eleven laws, none negotiable

  • The ground is near-black. Photography is black and white, hard sun, real blacks and blown whites; people in daylight are lit, with bright skin, squints, and crisp cast shadows.
  • Color is a lit event: few per frame, alive, never paint. The complete photographic rule set, "One wheel, the world lights it," rides at the bottom of the library.
  • Violet is the site accent. The other three bands exist and appear only when the content is theirs.
  • Cast light may land on hands, hair, fabric, and environment. A face takes a slight shine at most, never a mask; the scene makes the pop.
  • Drawn frequency is frozen everywhere until the wave lane lands. When it lands, waves come from code, never from generation.
  • No fills or panels over photography. Type sits on the stage ground; photos breathe alone.
  • Numbers are always Switzer. The serif never touches a digit.
  • The wordmark is the real asset, cream on dark, never re-typeset.
  • Gradients are linear and token-born. No radial glows, no gradient page grounds, no gradient text. Reading copy lives in the centered 700px column, left-aligned; centered text is for short headings and compact closers, never long copy.
  • No testimonial without a first and last name. Ever.
  • No charts anywhere until the site exists and the content is decided.
