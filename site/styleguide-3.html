01 · The World

Eleven laws, none negotiable

  • The ground is near-black. Photography is black and white, hard sun, real blacks and blown whites.
  • Color is an event, one per screen. It belongs to a person or a thing, never a wash.
  • Violet is the site accent. The other three colors exist and appear only when the content is theirs.
  • People in daylight are lit: bright skin, squints, crisp cast shadows. Full silhouettes are dusk grammar.
  • Waves are drawn by code, never generated. Wave E is the only wave. Water-3 is the only water.
  • No fills or panels over photography. Type sits on the stage ground, photos breathe alone.
  • Numbers are always Switzer. The serif never touches a digit.
  • The wordmark is the real asset, cream on dark, never re-typeset.
  • Gradients are linear and token-born. No radial glows, no gradient page grounds, no gradient text.
  • Reading copy lives in a 700px column. The 1140px grid is for composition, never for a line of text.
  • No charts anywhere until the site exists and the content is decided.

Sources: r19 parasol, r20 crosswalk, r21 rose-spin and four-caps-dive, wave dose E, water-embellish-3, design/tokens.json gradient recipes, plus the 30-frame keeper board.

03 · Gradients

Three recipes, all linear, all from tokens

The gradient system already exists in design/tokens.json; the site inherits it unchanged. Every gradient is linear, built from locked tokens, and lives on a graphic surface: a button, a drawn wave, a small identity element. The page ground never becomes a gradient, and nothing glows.

Functionalprimary → deep, same hue · #6521C9 → #38007A · buttons, controls, active states. ~90% of gradient use.
Frequencyprimary → gradEnd, analogous · #6521C9 → #A737C8 · a frequency's own identity: drawn waves, tiles. Each band has its own gradEnd.
Brandprimary → a different primary · hero moments, marketing identity only. Never inside app UI, and rare on the site.

This is the Signal voice: the frequency made visible. One wave per frame, whisper dose, smoke leading with a hairline filament as its spine. These are the tapped production references, shown the way the site will actually use them.

Use one: the frequency moment

A full-bleed frequency frame stands alone between content sections as a held breath. Caption below, nothing overlaid, one per page at most.

Use two: the filament divider

At interface scale the wave becomes a single code-drawn filament: an SVG whisper used as a section divider or a quiet signature near the footer. Hairline weight, violet, never animated to attract, never repeated down the page.

Filament · inline SVG · stroke 1.5px · violet-glint fading to nothing at both ends · one per page region · smoke-body waves stay in rendered frames, never in UI

Use three: the water treatment

Water · glint rides speculars only · one wave threads the dark · two voices may share a frame when classy

Bases come from photography; the wave layer is composited in code with numeric gates (identity untouched, hue in band, clearance held). Gen-drawn waves are banned by evidence: every attempt rendered neon.

06 · Paragraph Forms

Five ways a paragraph is allowed to exist

Every block of prose on the site is one of these five forms. The builder never invents a sixth.

1 · The lede

One paragraph directly under a headline, slightly larger, one idea, never more than two lines.

Lede · Switzer 400 · 23 / 1.55 desktop · 19 mobile · max 30em · one per section, optional

2 · The body paragraph

The workhorse. One to three sentences, then air. Two body paragraphs is the usual ceiling before an image, a list, or a quote breaks the column.

Body · Switzer 400 · 19 / 1.65 desktop · 17 / 1.6 mobile · max 34em · max 55 words

3 · The statement

One sentence the page wants remembered, set in the serif.

Statement · Cormorant 500 italic · 30 / 1.35 desktop · 24 mobile · max 12 words · max one per section

4 · The long-form paragraph

Only inside the 700px reading column (section 10): FAQ answers, story pages, policy pages. Same size as body, looser leading, and an H3 subhead every three to four paragraphs so the column never becomes a wall.

Long-form · Switzer 400 · 19 / 1.7 · reading column only · subhead every 3-4 paragraphs

5 · The note

Fine print, caveats, sources, legal. Quiet cream at forty percent, small, never load-bearing: if a sentence matters, it is not a note.

Note · Switzer 400 · 14 / 1.55 · cream 40% · caveats that must travel use cream 58%

10 · Long Copy and Short Copy

Two reading modes, two widths

Marketing sections are short copy: headline, two paragraphs, a list, a button, composed on the 1140px grid. Reading pages — FAQ answers, the story, policies — are long copy: a single 700px column where the type relaxes and subheads pace the scroll. Desktop text never runs wider than this column.

Long-form reading column · BANKED copy — canonical answer #28, confirmed
Support · Getting Set Up
Do I need headphones?

Headphones are preferred but not necessary. What matters is that you use a speaker that lets the frequency come through cleanly, which means clear midrange, balanced sound, and low distortion at normal volume.

This means that any speaker that costs about $75 or more (JBL Flip or Charge, Bose SoundLink, Sonos Roam or Move, Marshall, a mid-tier Anker Soundcore), almost any car stereo, or any decent pair of headphones will be fine. Apple EarPods are excellent.

What to avoid

Phone speakers, laptop speakers, and very small cheap Bluetooth speakers. They strip the low-end warmth, over-process the audio, flatten the sound, and lose detail.

Then just listen while you walk, work, cook, or drive. What matters is how much you listen over time.

Reading column · max 700px centered · body 19/1.7 · H3 subhead every 3-4 paragraphs · no photos wider than the column

Short copy is the assembled-section pattern in section 13: nothing in a marketing section ever exceeds the lengths table, and the whole section reads in under thirty seconds.

12 · FAQ

Questions in their voice, answers in ours

Questions are written the way customers ask them. On-site answers are the banked Short versions from the canonical answer bank; the full Long versions live on the support page. Both example answers below are BANKED and confirmed — the third is placeholder to show a closed state.

I don't know my exact birth time. Can I still use it?

This is our most common question, so you are not alone. The answer is that you can absolutely use the product even if you do not know your birth time. Your birth time does in fact fine-tune a part of how we calculate your frequencies.

However, depending on your configuration, a couple of minutes or even a couple of hours will not dramatically affect your calculation. The perfect frequency is better, but if it is in range, it will still have an amplifying effect.

More importantly, if you are not getting an experience, we will work with you through trial and error until you get an undeniable experience. This includes expanding your refund window, just make sure to communicate.

Do I need headphones?

Headphones, like Apple EarPods, are preferred. But you can use speakers, as long as you pick a model that lets the frequency come through cleanly, which means clear midrange, balanced sound, and low distortion at normal volume. Any Bluetooth speaker that costs $75 or more is totally acceptable. Your car stereo will work just fine, too!

Just not phone or laptop speakers. They blur everything together, so the tone gets lost.
