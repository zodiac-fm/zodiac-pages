A · The World in Color

The established style, at volume

The scene is black and white. The color pops out of it, lit, so it is art.

Eighty-one approved frames, the established style made explicit. The library grows: the R31 fix round and seven frames awaiting correction join as they pass.

  • The events are few. One band per frame is the default.
  • The color is lit, never paint. People light up, not fabric.
  • Light needs no visible source; an invented source is legal.
  • Both device grammars are legal: the colored object, and the black object under colored light.
  • Silhouettes are legal when the hue is ours and loud.
  • The multi-color grammar: different people may each carry a different band in one frame.
  • Community and connection scenes are Love rose, never Core violet.
  • Amber is the danger band: keep enough black and white in frame or the treatment reads as an ordinary color photo.
  • The Full Wheel appears as four objects, four people, or four lit events, never as a wash.

B · Light Casting

Light Casting: another treatment, used interchangeably with world color when appropriate

The world stays grey. A small window of light finds them, and it is alive.

The second voice in the photographic system, used alongside the established world-color style whenever the moment calls. Same black and white world, same locked wheel; the difference is dose and address. World color lets the world carry the hue. Light Casting closes it to a small vivid event landing on a person, and everything else stays unmistakably grey.

  • A small vivid event, everything else unmistakably grey. Wide color makes people look like they are already in color, and the read dies.
  • The light lands on people: faces and skin first, never a wash, never fabric doing the work.
  • Single-band windows: one scene, one band. Multi-band scenes: each person carries their own band.
  • The source can be invented or absent; the event must still read as light, alive, not paint.
  • Every frame audits onto the locked wheel spans, the same standing gate as the library above.
