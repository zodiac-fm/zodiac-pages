The Rules

One wheel, the world lights it

The scene is black and white. The color pops out of it, lit, so it is art.

Every category above is the same treatment, World of Color: the world carries the hue. A parasol, a dress, a lit sign, or cast light landing on hands, hair, fabric, the environment. Cast light is a color source inside the treatment, not a treatment of its own; the method is chosen frame by frame.

  • The events are few. One band per frame is the default.
  • The color is lit, never paint.
  • Cast light may land on faces, hands, hair, fabric, and environment. Never a pure mask, and never a frame where only faces carry the color: the scene makes the pop, in context.
  • Light needs no visible source; an invented or absent source is legal — the event must still read as light, alive, not paint.
  • Both device grammars are legal: the colored object, and the black object under colored light.
  • Silhouettes are legal when the hue is ours and loud.
  • Cast light stays a small vivid event: keep the world unmistakably black and white around it — wide color makes people look like they are already in color, and the read dies.
  • The multi-color grammar: the Full Wheel appears as four objects, four people, or four lit events, never a wash; in group scenes different people may each carry a different band.
  • Community and connection scenes are Love rose, never Core violet.
  • Amber is the danger band: keep enough black and white in frame or the treatment reads as an ordinary color photo.
